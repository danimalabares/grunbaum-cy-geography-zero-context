## 1. Exact all-equations theorem

Write A0=Z_(101), z=(w,y), with 21 free and 270 dependent normalized
invariant cubic coordinates. The original multiplication matrix has the
fixed decomposition mu=[[A,B],[C,D]], with A(0)=I98, B(0)=U0 and C(0)=D(0)=0.
Let E=D−CA^(-1)B. Work first over Q and complete at z=0.

For a noetherian local coefficient base T at this point satisfying E=0,
the matrix [-A^(-1)B; I30] gives lifted relations. In each degree d the
special kernel of the free generator map is generated by polynomial
multiples of their 30 reductions: this uses the *full* special resolution,
not only the dimension of the quartic kernel. Thus the reduction map
I_d tensor k → T[x]_d tensor k is injective. The exact sequence with the
free middle module gives Tor_1^T((T[x]/I)_d,k)=0. A finite module over a
noetherian local ring with this Tor vanishing is free. This identifies
the full Schur incidence formal functor with the Hilbert functor in the
normalized cubic chart. Conversely flatness fixes the degree-4 image
rank to 98, and the chosen unit pivot forces E=0; persistence of cubic
generation uses the accepted postulation/Nakayama statement.

The source complete obstruction group satisfies (T2)^S3=0. Averaging also
lifts equivariant frames, so this applies to embedded fixed deformations,
not merely abstract ones. Together with the verified full tangent rank
270 on 291 coordinates, this makes the full fixed Hilbert completion a
regular 21-dimensional ring. The selected 270 bordered equations have an
invertible dependent-variable Jacobian, and hence their quotient is
Q[[w1,...,w21]]. The full quotient is a quotient of this domain and is
regular of the same dimension. A nonzero ideal would lower its dimension;
therefore the kernel is zero. All omitted original E entries vanish.

The isotypic builder uses the trivial, sign, and a transposition-fixed
line in the standard representation. Restriction to that line detects
an equivariant map on the whole standard isotypic part. The averaged
section and saved bases give rational changes of basis from the original
multiplication map. Hence the full block Schur identities are equivalent
over Q to E=0. The three block A matrices are I at the origin. Replacing
a selected Schur entry by det(A_block) times that entry neither changes
its germ nor its differential there. This is the bridge from the actual
export to the original all-equations theorem. Integrality of the change
of basis itself is not additionally needed for the next argument: the
original E and the exported selected equations are already integral.

Now solve the actual selected subsystem over A0[[w]]. Its constant
Jacobian is a 101-unit (reported and separately replayed as 19 modulo
101); coefficient recursion uses only its fixed inverse and integral
polynomial operations. Every dependent coefficient is therefore integral.
Original A is I mod (w), so E evaluated on this solution belongs to
A0[[w]]. Its image in Q[[w]] is zero by the preceding paragraph. The
coefficientwise injection A0[[w]] → Q[[w]] proves E=0 already over A0.
No unverified assertion of a characteristic-101 universal Kuranishi
theorem, and no finite residual test, is needed here.

Put O=Z101[pi], pi^7=101, and substitute the original integral free paths
w_j=gamma_j(pi), each in pi O. Every series converges: a term of total
degree n has valuation at least n, and each degree has finitely many
terms. Evaluation is a ring homomorphism. Its dependent tuple solves the
exported subsystem and reduces to zero. Hensel uniqueness identifies it
with the tuple used to define the fibre. Thus all full Schur equations
vanish *exactly* in O. This argument does not assert their vanishing on
other global solutions of the selected subsystem.

## 2. Exact syzygies and preservation of the resolution

Set Delta=det(A), which lies in 1+pi O, and
K=[−adj(A)B; Delta I30], restoring the original multiplication-column
ordering. The upper block of mu K is zero by adjugation. The lower block
is Delta E and vanishes by C3. Defining
R_ik(x)=sum_j x_j K_(8(i−1)+j−1),k gives FR=0 exactly. Modulo pi the
30 columns are the canonical differences of equal central monomial
products; their extra-column coordinates are the identity. The accepted
resolution makes them generators of every special first syzygy.

For h=Fv in I cap pi O[x], reduce v modulo pi and write vbar=Rbar wbar.
Lift wbar polynomially to w. Then v−Rw=pi v1, hence h=pi Fv1. Therefore
I cap pi O[x]=pi I. The quotient M=O[x]/I is pi-torsion-free, thus O-flat,
and every graded piece is finite free. This proves the entire Hilbert
function, including the Hilbert series and polynomial in the packet.

Here is the full resolution induction omitted in the short reports.
The ideal I is O-flat, as a submodule of the O-flat polynomial ring.
Reduce 0→K1→O[x](−3)^16→I→0 modulo pi. Flatness of I gives exactness,
so K1/pi K1 is the complete central first-syzygy module. Lift its 30
degree-4 generators by R. The resulting free module surjects onto K1:
the cokernel vanishes degree by degree by finite-module Nakayama. K1 is
O-flat (it is torsion-free). Its next kernel therefore reduces to the
central next kernel. Repeat, lifting the 16 degree-5 and one degree-8
generators. The final remaining kernel has zero reduction and is zero
degree by degree. This constructs an exact relative graded resolution

0→S(−8)→S(−5)^16→S(−4)^30→S(−3)^16→S→M→0.

After inverting pi, the map degrees are respectively 3,1,1,3. No entry
has degree zero, so the resolution remains minimal and its ranks cannot
cancel. It gives a Cohen–Macaulay ring of codimension 4 and type one,
with a-invariant 0; hence Gorenstein with canonical module equal to the
ring. Saturation and the asserted O_X cohomology vanishings follow from
ACM and the same resolution. Field extension/descent transfers these
properties between L and Q101(pi). This conclusion does not use or prove
smoothness; smoothness still uses the separate transported witnesses.

## 3. Component bridge, including safe denominators

Let B denote the irreducible closure of the characteristic-zero fixed
formal germ in the coefficient/Hilbert chart. It is irreducible because
its defining ideal is the kernel of a map to Q[[w]], a domain. The
certified equivariant all-orders smoothing arc factors through this germ.
The generic Hilbert point of that arc is regular by the *independent*
smooth polarized Calabi–Yau deformation theorem (BTT, H1(O)=H2(O)=0,
extension of polarization, and frames). It therefore lies on exactly
one ambient Hilbert component C. Any component containing B contains
that point, so B lies in that C. This does not rely on the geography
argument's later H1(N)=0, and thus introduces no circular Hilbert-smoothness
assumption. One can carry out the same argument after algebraic closure;
the characteristic-zero smooth rational fixed germ is geometrically
irreducible at its origin.

Care is needed when evaluating local algebraic coordinates at pi.
SPARSE_AND_RAMIFIED_REVIEW §3 says that localized chart denominators have
unit constant terms and hence remain units. This is valid for the actual
integral matrix pivots with constant 1, but nonvanishing at the Q-origin
alone is insufficient. For example, 101−q^7 is invertible in Q[[q]] and
vanishes at q=pi. Thus arbitrary rational neighborhood localizations
must not be evaluated by that argument.

A concrete repair uses a standard projective Hilbert embedding. Choose
a sufficiently large degree m for the fixed Hilbert polynomial. At the
central monomial ideal, choose one first-occurring product x^alpha f_i
for each distinct monomial of I_m. Those products form an ideal basis;
use its coefficient rows as the Grassmann pivot. On the integral formal
family and on the actual O-family, the same minor is 1 modulo the
maximal ideal because the products reduce to those distinct monomials.
Its determinant is therefore a convergent integral series with constant
1 and a unit at pi. Flatness and Nakayama show the chosen products are
the full degree-m ideal basis on both families. Consequently their
normalized Grassmann coordinates, and the standard affine Hilbert
coordinates they induce, are given by finite polynomial/adjugate circuits
divided only by this determinant. These are exactly the denominators
needed; all are proved units, not merely declared nonzero at the Q-origin.

Take any polynomial equation h of C in this affine Hilbert chart. Its
pullback to the fixed formal family is zero. After clearing the finite
rational coefficient denominators of h, it is an identity of integral
convergent series. Evaluate at pi to obtain zero. The clearing integer
may be divisible by 101, but it is nonzero in the characteristic-zero
target field, so h itself vanishes. Thus the actual Hilbert point belongs
to C. This proves membership by exact all-orders component identities,
not by matching six jets or by reducing to the same special fibre.

There is no claim that the selected fibre lies on one predetermined
all-orders arc: the original existence proof did not distinguish one.
The bridge is membership in the same fixed germ and its containing
ambient component. Matching Hodge pairs or Hilbert polynomials is unused.
