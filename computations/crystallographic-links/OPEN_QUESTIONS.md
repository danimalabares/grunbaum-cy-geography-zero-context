## A. The row `(4,2)₁`: a compatible triangulation

**Missing.** A `(4,2)₁`-invariant rectilinear triangulation `K̃` of `C²` with regular action and simplicial
quotient (Arnoux–Marin's admissible diagonalisation). Proven here: none exists with the vertex-minimal
product cell structure on `E[2]×E[2]` (counting: 48 facets on 9 vertices is impossible for a closed
4-manifold with `χ = 3`); the `(4,1)₀`-lift `K̃₄` restricted to the index-4 conjugate `H` of `(4,2)₁` has a
non-simplicial quotient (doubled edges).

**Next computations.**
1. *Vertex-minimal counting for other cell structures.* For any `H`-invariant periodic cell decomposition
   with `c` 4-cells of normalised volumes `v_k` per fundamental domain of the translation lattice `Λ′`,
   a regular invariant diagonalisation has exactly `Σ v_k / 16` facets modulo `H` (`|G(4,2,2)| = 16`).
   Combine with the Brehm–Kühnel/Dehn–Sommerville constraints (`f₀ ≥ 9`; for `f₀ = 9` one needs 36 facets;
   for `f₀ = 10`, `f₄ = 48` is the only 2-neighbourly option, cf. `CP²₁₀`) to list the admissible
   `(f₀, f₄)` pairs before any search. A vertex set with 10 orbits and 48 facets would force `CP²₁₀`-type
   f-vectors; check first whether the ten Bagchi–Datta vertices can be `H`-marked at all (the odd permutation
   obstruction says the marking of `E[2]²` cannot be the swap-symmetric one).
2. *Add one free vertex orbit.* Take the vertex set `E[2]² ∪ (E[2]×B) ∪ (B×E[2]) ∪ B²` with `B` an
   `H`-orbit of regular points of `E` (e.g. the barycentres already used for `K̃₄`, or the square centres),
   build the `H`-invariant Delaunay or product cell structure, enumerate cell orbits and run the binary CSP
   of `scripts/four_two_one_search.py` with the fast precomputed vertex colouring and the necessary counting
   condition as a filter. Cost: the present search took 45 h because admissibility was tested only after
   full assignments; test the counting condition and the "distinct colours in every facet" condition on
   partial assignments instead.
3. *Use the `(2,1)₀ ◁ (4,2)₁` picture.* A `(4,2)₁`-admissible `K̃` restricts to a `(2,1)₀[τ=i]`-admissible
   one whose quotient is a 4-fold branched cover of the `(4,2)₁`-triangulation along the images of the three
   fixed lines `U=0, V=0, W=0`. Enumerate `(2,1)₀`-admissible triangulations with vertex set
   `E[2]² ∪ (extra orbits)` invariant under `diag(i,i)` and the diagonal half-period translation; the
   quotient by the extra `(Z/2)²` is then automatic (check regularity along the fixed lines).
4. *Delaunay approach.* The Delaunay subdivision of any `Γ`-invariant discrete point set is `Γ`-invariant
   (`Γ` acts by isometries). For `Γ = (4,2)₁` and the point set `(E[2]∪C)²` (the special points, `C` the
   centres) compute the Delaunay cells on the torus `C²/Λ` (they will be degenerate: the lattice is
   `D₄`-like) and classify their `H`-symmetric triangulations.

## B. Smoothings of the `CP²₁₀` links `L10a` (degree 27, `P⁸`) and `L10b` (degree 22, `P⁸`)

**Missing.** Any theorem, certificate or counterexample about smoothability of `Proj k[L10a]` and
`Proj k[L10b]` to Calabi–Yau threefolds; their Hodge numbers and Euler characteristics. Known here:
`T¹₀ = 54, T²₀ = 63` and `T¹₀ = 63, T²₀ = 21` (intrinsic, degree 0), invariant `T²₀ = 11` in both cases,
so the equivariant lifting lemma of the zero-context packet does not apply; degrees and Hilbert polynomials
`(9/2)k³ + (9/2)k`, `(11/3)k³ + (16/3)k`; conditional readings `c₂·L = 54, 64`.

**Next computations.**
1. *Versal family to order two.* `versalDeformation(F0, T1, T2, HighestOrder => 2)` in Macaulay2's
   `VersalDeformations` (as in `runs/cp2-nine-vertex-equivariant-t2-2026-09-09/scripts/quadric_rank_check.m2`)
   for both links: rank of the quadratic obstruction map `T²₀* → Sym²(T¹₀*)`; if some obstruction quadrics are
   nonzero, the SR point lies on several components or a singular locus of the Hilbert scheme. Expected cost:
   minutes (the ideals have 30 cubics / 5 quadrics + 10 cubics in 9 variables).
2. *Fausk-style invariant subfamilies.* Restrict to the `Aut`-invariant first-order directions (21 embedded,
   9 genuine for `L10a`; 68 and 32 for `L10b`) and compute the invariant versal family; the invariant `T²₀`
   is 11-dimensional in both cases, so obstructions may appear at order two; if the invariant family is
   unobstructed to high order, test smoothness of a generic fibre over `F_p` (Jacobian criterion on the
   affine charts, as in the packet's `p1_sparse_qadic_smoothness_probe_mod101.m2`).
3. *Component search by liaison/unprojection.* For `L10a` (arithmetically Gorenstein, codimension 5,
   h-vector `(1,5,15,5,1)`, ideal generated by 30 cubics): check whether `X` is a Tom/Jerry-type
   unprojection of a codimension-4 Gorenstein scheme (Coughlan–Gołębiowski–Kapustka–Kapustka methods), which
   would connect it to a known CY3 family in `P⁸`; compute the Betti table of the minimal free resolution
   first (Macaulay2, seconds).
4. *Nodal fibres.* Even when a smoothing fails, a fibre with isolated nodes and a small resolution would give
   a CY3 (Fausk's degree-11 case); locate first-order directions killing the fewest singularities by
   computing the singular locus of random first-order deformations over `F_p`.
5. *Polytopality of `L10a`, `L10b`.* Realise them as boundary complexes of 4-polytopes with 9 vertices (there
   are 1142 polytopal and 154 non-polytopal 9-vertex 3-spheres by Altshuler–Steinberg); polytopality is not
   needed for smoothing but is the natural first invariant to record for the "geography" of these links.

## C. The `(m,1)₀` rows: completeness of the construction

1. *Uniqueness of the vertex-minimal `E`-models.* The counts `f = (4,12,8), (6,18,12), (8,24,16), (12,36,24)`
   are forced, and the barycentric models are the natural ones, but uniqueness up to the normaliser of `Γ`
   was checked only for `m = 2` (by hand) and argued (not enumerated) for `m = 3,4,6`. Enumerate all
   `C_m`-invariant rectilinear triangulations of `E` with these vertex sets (finite: the vertex set is fixed,
   edges are lattice segments of bounded length).
2. *Non-minimal alternatives.* `K̃₄/(2,1)₀` and `K̃₄/H` are Δ-complexes, not simplicial; refining them (e.g.
   by barycentric subdivision of the doubled edges) gives larger `(2,1)₀`- and `(4,2)₁`-compatible
   triangulations. Compute their links and check whether any link is again one of `ℳ`, `L10a`, `L10b`.
3. *Real versus complex affine equivalence.* For generic `τ` the two Bagchi–Datta subdivisions pulled back to
   `C²` are exchanged only by a real-affine (`GL₂(Z)`) symmetry. Decide whether Galkin's intended
   compatibility (integral affine structure of the base of a Lagrangian fibration) is the real-affine or the
   complex-affine one; only in the latter case are the two `(2,1)₀`-lifts genuinely different.
