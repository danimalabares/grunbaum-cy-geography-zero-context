   * **Figure 2.8** (p. 10 of the PDF), caption "Singular sets for all 35 Euclidean 3-orbifolds that have S³

## C. The Kummer construction and its Hodge numbers

7. **M. Andreatta, J. A. Wiśniewski, *On the Kummer construction*, arXiv:0804.4611** (Rev. Mat. Complut.
   23 (2010)). Read (LaTeX source): §4 "Building upon elliptic curves", Proposition (classification of
   finite subgroups of SL(3,Z) up to GL(3,Z)-conjugacy, quoting Donten: dihedral D₂ₐ with 4,3,2,1 classes
   for a = 2,3,4,6; A₄: 3 classes; S₄: 3 classes), Lemma (fixed locus of a non-identity element of finite
   order is one-dimensional), §4.2 octahedral group and the Proposition `P_X(t) = t⁶+20t⁴+14t³+20t²+1`
   for a crepant resolution of A³/S₄; the hand construction of a global crepant resolution in
   dimension three (§3). Their statement that the Kummer variety has `K_X` trivial and `H¹(X,Z) = 0` is
   in the abstract.
8. **M. Donten-Bury, *On Kummer 3-folds*, arXiv:0812.3758** (Proc. Steklov Inst. Math. 2011). Read (LaTeX
   source `kummer.tex`): Introduction (A³ = Z³ ⊗_Z A, natural action of G < SL(3,Z) on Z³); §1
   classification: Lemma (isomorphism types), **Theorem (Table 1 lists one representative of each
   Z-class of finite subgroups of SL(3,Z))** and **Table 1** (16 non-cyclic classes with two generators
   each); **Proposition (duality)**: self-dual classes D4(1), D4(2), D6(3), D8(1), D8(2), D12, A4(1),
   S4(1); dual pairs (D4(3),D4(4)), (D6(1),D6(2)), (A4(2),A4(3)), (S4(2),S4(3)); §2 method
   (stratification and virtual Poincaré polynomials); §3–§4 the per-class tables; **Theorem (last) and
   Table 2** (Poincaré polynomials of the 16 Kummer 3-folds); the remark that dual classes have equal
   Poincaré polynomials and that D4(3), D4(4) differ by the connectedness of the singular locus of Y.
   Tables 1 and 2 were transcribed by parsing the source (`scripts/extract_literature.py`,
   `data/literature_zclasses.json`).
9. **D. Burek, *Zeta function of some Kummer Calabi–Yau 3-folds*, arXiv:2112.03970**. Read (LaTeX
   source): §2 (Chen–Ruan cohomology; Theorem quoted from Yasuda, "Cor. 3.16"), §3 (Kum₃(E,G) is
   Calabi–Yau iff `H^{1,0}(E³)^G = 0`; the 16 non-cyclic groups), §3.1 (the worked case G₂₄.₁, with
   `h^{1,1} = 1+4+1+4+10 = 20`, `h^{2,1} = 1+0+1+4+0 = 6`), §5 Examples (the other 15 groups with
   generators — Tahara's representatives — centraliser data, fixed loci and Hodge numbers). Generators
   and Hodge numbers transcribed by parsing the source (`data/literature_zclasses.json`). Burek's
   per-class centraliser orders and component counts were spot-checked against the present computation
   for G₂₄.₁ (= P432) and G₁₂.₁ (= P622): they agree.
10. **V. V. Batyrev, *Non-Archimedean integrals and stringy Euler numbers of log-terminal pairs*,
    arXiv:math/9803071** (J. Eur. Math. Soc. 1 (1999)). Read (source and text layer): Definition 3.1 and
    **Theorem 3.8** (the stringy E-function is independent of the log resolution; for a crepant
    resolution Y of a Gorenstein X it equals E(Y)), §7 Definition of the orbifold E-function
    `E_orb(V,G;u,v) = Σ_{[g]} Σ_{[W]} (uv)^{wt(g,W)} E(W/C(g,W);u,v)` (sum over conjugacy classes and
    C(g)-orbits of connected components W of V^g), the definition of the Kawamata log terminal pair
    (X, Δ_X) associated with (V,G) (Δ_X supported on the branch divisor, zero when no element fixes a
    divisor), and **Theorem 7.5**: `E_st(X, Δ_X; u,v) = E_orb(V,G; u,v)` for any finite group G acting
    regularly on a smooth algebraic variety V. Also Theorem 1.10 / 8.4 (local case).
11. **T. Yasuda, *Twisted jets, motivic measures and orbifold cohomology*, arXiv:math/0110228** (Compos.
    Math. 140 (2004)). Read (source and text layer): **Theorem 1.5 (= Corollary 3.16)**: "Let X and X′ be
    complete varieties with Gorenstein quotient singularities. Suppose that there are proper birational
    morphisms Z → X and Z → X′ such that K_{Z/X} = K_{Z/X′}. Then the orbifold cohomologies of X and X′
    have the same Hodge structures"; Theorem 3.15 (the shift `s(Y)` = age on each component of the
    inertia stack).
12. **T. Bridgeland, A. King, M. Reid, *The McKay correspondence as an equivalence of derived
    categories*, arXiv:math/9908027** (J. Amer. Math. Soc. 14 (2001)). Read (source): setting "M a
    non-singular quasi-projective complex variety of dimension n, G ⊂ Aut(M) a finite group such that
    ω_M is locally trivial as a G-sheaf, X = M/G, Y the irreducible component of G-Hilb(M) containing
    the free orbits"; **Theorem 1.1** (if dim Y ×_X Y ≤ n+1 then Y is a crepant resolution of X and Φ is
    an equivalence) and **Theorem 1.2** ("Suppose n ≤ 3. Then G-Hilb(M) is irreducible and is a crepant
    resolution of X").
13. **J. Denef, F. Loeser, arXiv:math/9903187** — cached, not used (Batyrev and Yasuda suffice).
14. **K. Hashimoto, A. Kanazawa, *Calabi–Yau threefolds of type K (I)*, arXiv:1409.7601**. Read
    (source, Introduction and §4): a Calabi–Yau threefold is a smooth projective threefold with trivial
    canonical bundle and `H¹(X,O_X) = 0`; if `π₁(X)` is infinite, X has an étale Galois covering by an
    abelian threefold (**type A**) or by the product of a K3 surface and an elliptic curve (**type K**);
    exactly eight type-K deformation classes. Used only to note that type A/K require infinite `π₁`.
15. **M. Fischer, M. Ratz, J. Torrado, P. K. S. Vaudrevange, *Classification of symmetric toroidal
    orbifolds*, arXiv:1209.3906** (JHEP 2013). Read (source): §5 tables: Q-classes with numbers of
    Z-classes (six-dimensional lattices) and affine classes — S₃: 6 Z-classes/11 affine classes,
    D₄: 9/48, A₄: 9/15, D₆: 2/8, S₄: 6/19; Table comparing the Z₂×Z₂ affine classes with
    Donagi–Wendland and Förste et al. Their Z-classes are six-dimensional; the matching of the present
    L = Λ ⊕ τΛ with diagonal action to their classes needs their lattice data and is **pending**
    (see `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`).
