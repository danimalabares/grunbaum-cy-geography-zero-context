## Exact Hilbert data

The special coordinate ring has (h)-vector

\[
(1,4,10,4,1)
\]

and minimal resolution

\[
0\to S(-8)\to S(-5)^{16}\to S(-4)^{30}
\to S(-3)^{16}\to S\to S/I_M\to0.
\]

The exact checks give projective dimension three and degree 20. Expanding
the Hilbert series gives

\[
\operatorname{Hilb}_{S/I_M}(z)=
\frac{1+4z+10z^2+4z^3+z^4}{(1-z)^4},\qquad
P(m)=\frac{10}{3}m^3+\frac{14}{3}m.
\]

These are unconditional consequences of the Stanley--Reisner ideal and are
preserved by any flat family with this special fiber.

## What 109 and 53 mean

The number 109 is the dimension of the embedded Hilbert tangent space
(operatorname{Hom}_S(I_M,S/I_M)_0). The infinitesimal coordinate action
has image dimension 56, not 63, because the monomial point has a
seven-dimensional stabilizer inside (PGL_8). The selected 53 columns are a
linear complement to this 56-dimensional orbit tangent. They are not a
second computation of the Hilbert tangent space and are not a canonical
quotient basis.

## Quadratic cone versus actual Hilbert components

The 27 quadratic Kuranishi forms involve 36 intrinsic variables. They split
into three disjoint 12-variable blocks. Each block has three minimal primes,
so the quadratic obstruction locus has 27 components. Adding the 17
quadratically free intrinsic coordinates and then the 56 coordinate-orbit
directions gives:

| number of top Segre factors | quadratic components | intrinsic dimension | embedded dimension |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 1 | 38 | 94 |
| 2 | 6 | 37 | 93 |
| 1 | 12 | 36 | 92 |
| 0 | 8 | 35 | 91 |

These are components and dimensions of the quadratic initial obstruction
locus, not established components of the completed local Hilbert scheme.
Higher equations may shrink, merge, obstruct, or eliminate branches. What
is established from the initial equations is the upper bound

\[
\dim_{[X_M]}\operatorname{Hilb}(\mathbf P^7)\le94.
\]

The source proof establishes existence of at least one smoothing component,
but does not determine its dimension, the number of smoothing components,
or which of the 27 quadratic components contain genuine full branches.

At a smooth Calabi--Yau fiber (Y), BTT unobstructedness, unique extension
of (H), and projective framing give a smooth embedded Hilbert germ of
dimension

\[
63+h^{2,1}(Y).
\]

Therefore every smoothing component through (X_M) has smooth-fiber
dimension at most 94 and (h^{2,1}(Y)\le31).

## Smooth-fiber numerical consequences

For a smooth nearby fiber, semicontinuity and the Gorenstein/ACM special
fiber give (h^1(\mathcal O_Y)=h^2(\mathcal O_Y)=0) and
(h^0(\mathcal O_Y)=h^3(\mathcal O_Y)=1). Comparing the Hilbert polynomial
with threefold Riemann--Roch first gives

\[
H^3=20,\qquad K_Y\cdot H^2=0.
\]

Serre duality provides a nonzero section of (K_Y). Ampleness of (H)
forces its divisor to be empty, so (K_Y\cong\mathcal O_Y). The linear
coefficient then gives

\[
c_2(Y)\cdot H=56.
\]

Kodaira vanishing yields (h^0(Y,H)=P(1)=8). Openness of nondegeneracy
shows that the eight ambient sections remain independent, hence the
embedding is the complete system (|H|).

The exact Hodge pair and Euler characteristic remain unknown. The only
current constraints are

\[
h^{1,1}\ge1,\qquad h^{2,1}\le31,\qquad
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}=2(h^{1,1}-h^{2,1})\ge-60.
\]

Because (20) is not divisible by any cube (n^3) for (n>1), the
polarization is primitive: (H=nL) in `Pic(Y)` would imply
(20=n^3L^3), impossible.

No source argument determines whether `Pic(Y)=ZH`, whether torsion is
present, or whether (Y) is simply connected. Those questions need new
topological input or an explicit smooth model.

## External comparison: known degree-20 families

This paragraph is literature evidence, not a deduction from the packet.
Coughlan--Gołębiowski--Kapustka--Kapustka list the then-known smooth aG
degree-20 family as the (3\times3) minors of a (4\times4) matrix of
linear forms, with Hodge numbers ((h^{1,1},h^{2,1})=(2,34)); they present
completeness in degree 20 as evidence/problem rather than a proved
classification ([arXiv:1609.01195](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01195), Table
1 and Problem 3.1). Bertin is the primary construction source
([arXiv:math/0701511](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0701511)). The later
quaternary-quartic study again identifies smooth degree-20 examples of this
Betti type with (4\times4) submaximal-minor liftings, while showing that
general Artinian data of the same type need not lift smoothly in dimension
three ([arXiv:2111.05817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05817), Introduction
and Section 8 discussion).

The known determinantal family has smooth embedded Hilbert dimension
(63+34=97). Under the explicit hypothesis used above—that the
`VersalDeformations` output is the full embedded Kuranishi obstruction
output—the local dimension is at most 94. Under that hypothesis, the general
determinantal family cannot contain this point. The matching Betti table is
therefore not a family identification. If both the zero-context smoothing
proof and this Kuranishi-output hypothesis hold, the smoothing lies on a
different degree-20 component from the known determinantal component.

This makes exact determination of (h^{1,1}), (h^{2,1}), and the
Artinian-reduction/apolar stratum especially important: it is a
discriminator, not cosmetic bookkeeping.

## Linkage and liaison

The inspected degree-20 classification entry is determinantal, whereas the
same table explicitly labels several degrees 17--19 as obtained by
bilinkage. No primary source inspected here gives a rational linkage for the
new component forced by the (94)-dimensional bound. A matching resolution
does not transfer the known determinantal geometry.

An explicit liaison to a rational threefold is therefore open. To establish
one, one needs finite equations for a smooth member (or an algebraic generic
member), an explicit ambient arithmetically Gorenstein scheme, exact colon
ideal identities for each link, and preservation/control of smoothness and
the intended component. Abstract Gorenstein-liaison statements would not by
themselves identify a rational residual threefold or the deformation family.

## What follows from which input

- From the special fiber and flatness alone: Hilbert polynomial, degree, and
  the fixed Hilbert function.
- From flatness plus smoothness and standard vanishing/Riemann--Roch:
  (K_Y=0), (H^3=20), (c_2\cdot H=56), (h^0(H)=8), complete
  linear-system embedding, and primitive (H).
- From the local Kuranishi bound plus smooth CY deformation theory:
  (h^{2,1}\le31) and exclusion of the known 97-dimensional determinantal
  component.
- Not determined without further work: exact Hodge numbers, Euler number,
  Picard lattice/rank/torsion, simple connectedness, smoothing-component
  count, a finite algebraic model, and a rational liaison construction.
